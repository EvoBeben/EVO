04 / 10What decided the week

Backdrop

Spending rewarded where the revenue showed — and only there

Backdrop

what set the tone

The capex split

Microsoft and Amazon both raised what they intend to spend and rose 21.4% and 16.9% on the week. Meta and Apple guided softly and fell 9% and 7.1%. The market is no longer judging the spending — it is judging whether the revenue has already arrived.

Three dissents

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75%, but Hammack, Kashkari and Logan each wanted a quarter-point hike. Three dissents in the same direction is the first since September 2016 — a hold that reads more hawkish than the decision itself.

Yields at 2007 highs

The 30-year Treasury reached 5.25%, its highest since 2007, and the 10-year 4.73%, the highest since January 2025. Equities rose through it anyway, which is the part that should not be taken for granted.

A hundred-year flood

Tim Cook described memory pricing as “a hundred-year flood”. The memory squeeze that has run through this sheet for three weeks stopped being a chip-sector story and started showing up in Apple’s cost line — and took roughly $475bn of its market value with it.

Intel’s month

Intel closed July down 33.74%, its worst month since September 2000 and the month’s biggest casualty — a fortnight after beating on revenue and EPS and being sold for its capex anyway.

July closed up

Despite all of it, all three indexes finished the week higher and the Dow gained 0.7% across July — a fourth straight winning month. The volatility was inside the index, not in it.
